# Career depth and scramble update

The career audit identified inflated overall ratings, disappearing season customizations, volume-driven XP, early attribute caps, static rosters, weak draft consequences and misleading league comparisons.

## Changes
- QB attributes are explicitly accuracy, arm, release and speed. Overall is their mean. Loading a legacy save repairs overall without removing earned attributes or points.
- Upgrades add up to two, cost more at high ratings, and favor the archetype specialty. XP thresholds gradually reach 200. Rewards emphasize efficiency, ball security, scoring and rushing; objectives rotate every week.
- Tuck & Run commits to a scramble before or after the snap. Existing steering and jukes apply. No pass after tucking. Speed changes movement; rushing stats, touchdowns, sidelines, tackle animation and defense pursuit apply.
- Mentoring spends three points on a teammate's overall/attributes once per week, between games.
- Draft round sets rookie expectations, coach confidence and contract length. Players always start. Results change confidence, which affects renewal length; renewals remain automatic.
- Offseasons preserve team branding/uniforms, archive regular-season results, award milestones, age/develop/decline teammates, decrement contracts, replace retirees with rookies, and move a limited set of same-position free agents. Coaches renew automatically.
- Regular-season and playoff leaderboards are separate; completion rankings require qualifying attempts. Simulated targets favor talent; league possessions scale with quarter length.
- Career story includes season history, college awards and offseason news. Recaps show goal results, draft movement and confidence. Pause includes current objective progress.

## Intended boundaries
This is a QB career, not a full franchise manager. There is no salary cap, manual contract negotiation, user-team trade request, injury system or bench simulation. Scrambling is a committed run, not free movement while passing. Existing elite careers retain their attributes; mentoring provides another use for points. Automatic roster movement preserves player IDs and historical statistics. Physical iPhone Safari testing remains separate from browser phone-size emulation.

## Verification
Career unit/engine checks cover legacy rating repair, exact capped upgrades, season persistence, teammate development, objective rotation, XP efficiency, scrambling movement and rushing touchdown accounting. Existing career/college/save/gameplay tests and production build are run alongside phone-size browser tests. A multi-season simulation checks pacing, save reloads and roster identity uniqueness.
